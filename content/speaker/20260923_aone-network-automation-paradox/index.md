Everyone says network engineers need to automate. Learn Python. Learn Ansible. Build a source of truth. Learn Git. Test your changes. Stop doing everything manually.

There's just one problem: when?

At AutoCon 5 in Munich, Andy Lapteff sat down with Dutch network automation consultants Bart Dorlandt and Bart Smeding for a conversation that starts with Python and eventually lands somewhere much more interesting: time.

Automation can turn three hours of work into three minutes. But getting there requires engineers to invest time they often don't have, or don't feel permitted to protect. Andy knows that firsthand — at one point in his career his manager gave him an entire day every week to learn automation, and he kept spending that day catching up on operational work instead.

That question opens a much bigger conversation about engineering culture, burnout, professional identity, European and American attitudes toward work, change freezes, technical debt, and the endless tension between keeping today's network running and building a better way to operate it tomorrow.

Andy wrote up his side of it in a companion blog post: [My Boss Gave Me Tuesdays. I Gave Them Back.](https://artofnetworkengineering.com/blog/my-boss-gave-me-tuesdays-i-gave-them-back)
