🎉 5 years of Dream Networking and Automation (and a bit, I'm late to my own party). What a journey it's been.

For the past 5 years, running Dream Networking and Automation has meant one thing above all: helping teams find a better way to run their networks — through architecture, automation, and training, without a vendor agenda pulling the conversation in one direction.

No badge to defend. No quota to hit. Just the best solution for the problem in front of me. That freedom is still, five years in, the best part of the job.

It started in July 2021:

🔹 Went all-in as a freelance Network Automation Solution Architect

Starting this journey was a somewhat thought-out plan, though I had no idea where it would lead or what adventures would lie ahead. This included having a lot of conversations with peers and companies, figuring out what my next steps would be. And, honestly, some fear of the unknown.

Since the start I've worked with several companies and projects, each with their own challenges and fun. Just a few highlights of this period:

🔹 Having 7 interviews before landing the first project<br/>
🔹 Joining a startup in California during Covid<br/>
🔹 Witnessing a startup fail<br/>
🔹 Having 0 interviews before landing a project<br/>
🔹 Deploying multiple tech stacks in production on a worldwide scale for configuration and monitoring<br/>
🔹 Switching between companies/projects and the different maturity levels for each<br/>
🔹 Going from mouse management to automated workflows<br/>
🔹 Working on 15-year-old automation and the challenges for lifecycle management around it<br/>

Different companies, different challenges, different learning experiences — that's been the theme of the past 5 years. You never know what to expect, which makes it all the more exciting and rewarding.

Alongside that, I've become more active in the community, mainly in the last 2 years:

🔹 Grew my LinkedIn presence<br/>
🔹 Wrote blog posts<br/>
🔹 Presented at events on Network Automation and Python<br/>
🔹 Joined podcasts as a guest<br/>

The real fun has been the side quests: I became a co-host for PyUtrecht and started my own event, NLNAM (NL Network Automation Meetup). This year I was selected for the Network Automation Forum Advisory Board.

Five years ago I bet on "there must be a better way." Turns out that bet keeps paying off — not just in projects delivered, but in a community I get to help build.

Here's to the next 5. 🥂
